Computational / in-silico research

Computational Drug Repurposing Against Drug-Resistance-Associated Proteins of Leishmania tropica

Undergraduate thesis investigating potential efflux pump inhibitors against drug-resistance-associated proteins through protein analysis, structural modelling, molecular docking, ADMET assessment and molecular dynamics simulations.

Oct 2025–Feb 2026Writeup in progress
Conceptual illustration · not experimental or manuscript data
01 / Research objective

Research question

Investigate potential efflux pump inhibitors against drug-resistance-associated proteins of Leishmania tropica through a connected bioinformatics and structural-analysis workflow.

02 / My contribution

What I did

I independently carried out the protein analysis, structural modelling and validation, molecular docking, interaction analysis and ADMET assessment. The project also includes completed molecular dynamics simulations; these were not performed independently by me.

  • Worked from whole-genome shotgun data to identify and characterize potential protein targets through functional annotation, sequence similarity analysis and physicochemical profiling.
  • Generated protein structures through homology modelling and performed structural refinement and validation using SWISS-MODEL, GalaxyRefine, ERRAT and PROCHECK.
  • Prepared candidate compounds, performed molecular docking using AutoDock Vina and analysed ligand–protein interactions using BIOVIA Discovery Studio.
  • Evaluated compound drug-likeness, pharmacokinetic properties and toxicity predictions using SwissADME and pkCSM.
03 / Computational tools

Tools I used & learned

  • Protein annotation and analysis: InterProScan, BLASTp and ProtParam.
  • Homology modelling and refinement: SWISS-MODEL and GalaxyRefine.
  • Structural validation: ERRAT and PROCHECK.
  • Molecular docking: AutoDock Vina.
  • Ligand–protein interaction analysis: BIOVIA Discovery Studio.
  • Drug-likeness, pharmacokinetic and toxicity assessment: SwissADME and pkCSM.

Computational predictions require experimental validation.
